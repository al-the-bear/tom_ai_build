**How to write.**
- State the idea as it stands: what the system is and does, in the present tense, in the Project Idea's own words — not a history of how it was decided, and no references to todos. Plain, complete sentences; lists and tables where the content is a list or a comparison.
- Every statement the Solution Blueprint will need to cite carries an id: a capability `C<area>.<n>` (numbered per area, in the order written), a non-functional expectation `N-<n>`, a decision `D-<n>`, a contradiction `X-<n>`, a risk `R-<n>`, a deferred feature `L-<n>`. After a statement, name its source in brackets: the source id and the place, e.g. `(S1 § 7.3, DQ-2)`.
- What the sources state as decided is written as fact. What a source only proposes (the concept marks it **Proposal**) is written as a capability and gets a confirmation question. What the sources leave open, contradict each other on, or do not say although the Blueprint will need it, becomes a question — do not assume.
- **Questions** go into `new_questions.md`, under a heading naming the section id they belong to, not into the document. Form: header `> **❓ PQ-<n> — <topic>:** <question>` (take the next free number in the file), then inside the same quote a **Context** paragraph (quote the text it refers to in full), a **Why I ask** paragraph, the options `> - (a) …`, `> - (b) …` each with what it means in practice, the recommended one bold (`> - **(b)** …`), a `> *Recommendation:*` line with the reason, and `> **Answer:**`. A question stands on its own: it names nothing by an id alone. Ask only what really blocks or shapes the Blueprint; a detail the Blueprint's own rounds will settle is not asked now.
- References: within the document by section-id chain, outer first (`TAC > TAC-TEAMS`), never broken across lines; to another document by its path and section.
- A remark that especially needs the reviewer starts with `___check here___` and says why. Sign what you write into the document as `agent_spec_author` where a signature applies.
